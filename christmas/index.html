Christmas Escape Room at Home for Adults: The Twelve O'Clock Parcel

A free, complete printable escape room for two adults. Four sealed cards, one four-digit code, about 10–15 minutes at the table, or 30–45 if you hide the cards around the house. Print it, hide the seals, and open the parcel together. No signup, no app, nothing to buy.

This is the whole room. All four puzzles and the answer key are on this page. Nothing is held back behind an email address or a purchase.

Before you start (about 10 minutes)

  1. Print this page — it comes out as two or three sheets. The four seals are laid out to print in black and white on a home printer.
  2. Wrap something small — a chocolate orange, a note, a photograph, whatever suits the evening — and set it aside as the parcel.
  3. Write the four-digit code (it's in the answer key at the bottom) on a gift tag and tie it to the parcel, so the code is what opens it.
  4. Optional, and it's what makes it a room instead of a worksheet: cut the four seals apart and hide seals two, three and four around one or two rooms, using each answer as the hiding place for the next one — see the hiding map below.

The hiding map (optional)

Every seal's answer is a number, so every answer can name the next hiding place. Write down where you put everything before you forget — hiding clues too well is the single most common way an at-home escape room stalls.

  • Seal One's answer is a position in a stack. Hide Seal Two that many items down in a pile — books, cushions, folded blankets.
  • Seal Two's answer is a digit. Hide Seal Three behind that many ornaments along a shelf or a branch.
  • Seal Three's answer is a digit. Hide Seal Four in a drawer that many down from the top.

The setup

A parcel arrived this morning with no sender on it, wrapped tight and tied with four paper seals. The tag says only that it doesn't open until the number is found. Break the four seals in order, and the last one gives you the code.

Seal One

The Stack Under the Tree

Four wrapped parcels are stacked one on top of another. One is gold, one green, one red, one silver.

  • The silver parcel is at the very bottom of the stack.
  • The gold parcel sits directly on top of the green one.
  • The red parcel is not at the top.

Counting from the top down, what position is the green parcel in? That's your first digit.

Seal Two

The Advent Calendar

An advent calendar has 24 windows, numbered 1 to 24. Someone impatient has already opened every window whose number is a multiple of 3.

How many windows are still shut? Take the last digit of that number.

Seal Three

The Frosted Window

A word has been written on the glass as numbers. Each number is that letter's place in the alphabet — so 1 is A, 2 is B, and so on.

19 · 14 · 15 · 23

Work out the word. How many letters does it have? That's your next digit.

Seal Four

The Note on the Mantelpiece

Someone left a short list by the fire. Read only the first letter of each line:

Nutmeg in the cocoa,
Ivy on the banister,
Needles from the tree,
Embers in the grate.

The letters spell a number. Write that number as a digit — it's the last one.

Fourth digit:    

The Parcel

Put your four digits together in seal order. That's the number on the tag — go open the parcel.

Answer key (don't open this until you're stuck, or unless you're the one setting it up)

Seal One. Silver is fourth. Gold sits directly on green, so that pair is either 1–2 or 2–3. If it were 2–3 the red parcel would have to be first, and red is not at the top. So gold is first, green second, red third, silver fourth. Green is in position 2.

Seal Two. The multiples of 3 up to 24 are 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21 and 24 — eight windows opened, so 24 − 8 = 16 still shut. Last digit: 6.

Seal Three. 19 = S, 14 = N, 15 = O, 23 = W, spelling SNOW — four letters. Third digit: 4.

Seal Four. N, I, N, E spells NINE. Fourth digit: 9.

The code is 2–6–4–9.

Questions people ask

Is this really free, or is half of it behind an email?
Really free, and complete. Every puzzle and the full answer key are on this page. There is no signup and no account.
Is it for adults or for kids?
It's written for two adults on a quiet evening, and it's strictly PG. If you want one for a children's party, our sister site has printable escape room kits for ages 4–9.
How long does it take?
About 10–15 minutes at the table, or 30–45 if you hide the cards around the house, including setup.
What do we need?
A printer, two or three sheets of paper, scissors, and something to use as the parcel. No locks, batteries or props to buy.
Can we play it on a normal evening in December?
Yes. Nothing in the puzzles depends on the 25th, so it works across the whole season.
